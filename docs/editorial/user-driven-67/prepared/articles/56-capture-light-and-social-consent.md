# A Capture Light Does Not Replace a Conversation About Recording

A [traveller's Ray-Ban Meta account](https://www.reddit.com/r/RaybanMeta/comments/1uzd2t2/people_who_cover_the_led_ruin_it_for_everyone_else/) describes another person feeling uncomfortable around camera glasses and the wearer putting them away. Lensden cannot authenticate the other person's prior experiences from that account. What is directly relevant is the reported social interaction and the wearer's response.

An indicator can communicate device state, but it does not guarantee that everyone recognises the product or understands the signal. Winning an online argument about a light's meaning is a poor substitute for explaining what is happening to someone in the room.

## Explain the intended action before capture

Where recording is appropriate, tell people what you intend to record and why. Respect a request not to participate and the venue's rules. Avoid recording sensitive settings or private material merely because the device is convenient. Applicable recording rules depend on the situation and location; this article does not offer a legal permission to record.

The [manufacturer's responsible-innovation information](https://www.meta.com/actions/responsible-innovation/) describes user controls and the broader privacy context. Keep capture indicators intact and use the documented device behaviour. This article provides no instructions for concealing, disabling or bypassing them.

## Distinguish recording from listening features

A wearer may use glasses for audio or an assistant without intending to take a photograph. That does not mean another person knows the state. Be willing to explain the function, remove the glasses or use another device when the setting makes that appropriate.

Do not promise that an unlit capture light independently proves no microphone processing, stored voice interaction or connected-app activity. Those are separate questions to review against the exact product's current privacy information. A photograph of the glasses cannot settle them.

## Review sharing as a second decision

Permission to capture a moment is not automatically permission to publish it to a public feed. Before sharing, consider the people, location and incidental information in the recording, and inspect the actual audience selected in the app. Avoid using a private interaction as promotional material without appropriate permission.

For a purchase, ask whether the product can be used comfortably within the wearer's real social settings, not simply whether it records attractive video.
